# I founded southeastern Mexico's leading hackathon from scratch

Anchor metric: 3,000+ cumulative participants (2018-2020). Estimate, not additive with other portfolio metrics.

Role: Founder & Innovation Program Director.

Demonstrated capabilities: ecosystem development · innovation program design · strategic alliances · program leadership.

Context: HackSureste, own initiative · since October 2018 · locations in Mérida and Ciudad del Carmen · participants from 6 states.

## Context

In 2018, southeastern Mexico had talent, but no clear door into the innovation ecosystem.

Anyone who wanted to build something different usually had to leave the region to find community, methodology, and allies. The energy existed. What was missing was the system to focus it.

I founded HackSureste with no sponsoring brand behind it, no inherited budget, and no prior format to copy. It had to be built from zero.

Organizing a hackathon was the easy part. The hard part was making sure it didn't die after the first edition. The region needed more than a weekend event: it needed an innovation institution with repeatable methodology, active allies, and enough community to sustain itself between editions.

Then 2020 arrived and the problem changed shape: an event designed to bring people together in a room had to keep existing in the middle of a pandemic. The challenge stopped being event production. It became operational continuity.

> The region needed more than a weekend event: it needed an innovation institution.

## Action

I was responsible for:

- Founding and directing the program from the first edition.
- Designing the open innovation project strategy.
- Building alliances with universities, government, and the private sector.
- Designing and producing the virtual experiences from 2020 to 2022.
- Facilitating the editions and hosting the sessions as master of ceremonies.

I was not responsible for:

- The technical development of participants' projects.
- Investment or follow-on funding for the teams.
- Judges' decisions.

System built:

1. I designed a repeatable format, not a one-off event. HackSureste was replicated in Mérida and Ciudad del Carmen with the same methodology.
1. I built a regional network of allies with universities, government, and the private sector. That network sustained the program before, during, and after each edition.
1. I adapted the program to digital formats once in-person delivery stopped being viable. HackSureste WIRE brought together 6 free conferences and workshops online between March and April 2020, in collaboration with RushFrame.
1. I extended the program with ideaLab, a post-hackathon support space. A hackathon generates ideas in 48 hours; ideaLab existed for what happened after: training, validating, and connecting teams with industry experts.
1. I attracted participation from 6 states, turning a local initiative into a regional platform.

Trade-off: I kept HackSureste as an independent initiative instead of turning it into a single brand's asset. That made operations harder. The budget was tighter and every edition required re-coordinating resources, allies, and narrative from scratch. But that independence was also the reason the program could bring government, universities, companies, and the entrepreneurial community to the same table. A hackathon with a corporate owner doesn't get that table.
