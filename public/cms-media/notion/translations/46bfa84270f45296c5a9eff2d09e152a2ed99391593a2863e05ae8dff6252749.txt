# HackSureste: Building a Program That Could Continue Beyond the First Event

In October 2018, I founded HackSureste to connect talent from southeastern Mexico with a community, a methodology, and partners. The challenge was to build a program that could sustain itself between events.

I started without a sponsoring brand behind me, without an existing budget, and without a previous format to replicate. I had to secure partners, design the experience, and coordinate everything needed to make it happen again.

Role: Founder & Innovation Program Director.

Locations: Mérida and Ciudad del Carmen.

Skills: ecosystem development, innovation program design, strategic partnerships, and program management.

## Context

HackSureste was born out of the lack of a clear gateway into the region’s innovation ecosystem. Those who wanted to develop their ideas needed to find people to work with, a methodology, and access to partners.

The first edition brought together nearly 250 participants. To ensure its continuity, we needed a format we could replicate, active partners, and a community that would maintain connections between events.

In 2020, another challenge arose. We had designed an experience to bring people together in a room, but we had to keep the program running during the pandemic. It was time to adapt our operations.

## My Role

I founded and led the program from its very first edition. I designed the strategy for the open innovation projects and built partnerships with universities, the government, and the private sector. I also facilitated the events, served as master of ceremonies, and designed and produced virtual experiences from 2020 to 2022.

My responsibilities had specific limits: the technical development of the projects, their subsequent funding, and the judges’ decisions were beyond my purview.

## What I Built

1. A format we could replicate. I designed the hackathon methodology, and we replicated it in Mérida and Ciudad del Carmen. This allowed us to continue the program at different locations.
1. A regional network of partners. I connected universities, government, and the private sector to support the program before, during, and after each edition.
1. An alternative to continue online. Between March and April 2020, HackSureste WIRE hosted six free conferences and workshops in collaboration with RushFrame. We adapted the experience when in-person meetings were no longer feasible.
1. Post-hackathon support. I expanded the program with ideaLab to train the teams, help them validate their proposals, and connect them with industry experts. The ideas that emerged during the 48-hour event required further development.
1. Regional participation. The program welcomed participants from six states, as reported in the project profile. This information still requires supporting documentation.

## The Decision and Its Cost

I kept HackSureste as an independent initiative. That decision allowed us to bring together government, universities, companies, and the entrepreneurial community around the program.

Honestly, it also made operations more difficult. We worked with a tighter budget, and each edition required us to coordinate resources, partners, and communication all over again. Independence came at a cost that had to be borne with each edition.

## What I Failed to Build

I never turned the participant database into an operational asset. Although the program reports an estimated 3,000+ cumulative participants, I didn’t establish a community I could engage with the very next day.

Each edition had to rebuild its audience from scratch through marketing. The relationship with those who had already participated didn’t sustain the next call for applications. That was a systemic error.

The participant base should have been part of the program’s work from the start, with a way to maintain contact and ensure the relationship’s continuity.

If you’re building a program of this type, define how you’ll stay in touch with participants before opening the next call for applications.

The relationship also requires ongoing management.
