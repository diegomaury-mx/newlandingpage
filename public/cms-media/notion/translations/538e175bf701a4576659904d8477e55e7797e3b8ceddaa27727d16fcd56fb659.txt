## Background

In 2015, the COPARMEX Youth Laguna Chapter lacked a formal channel connecting the business sector and universities. Student chapters were sporadic, with no operating rules or way to measure whether a chapter was actually functioning. I joined as Vice President of Outreach in La Laguna (2015–2016) and later held two more vice-presidential positions in Mérida: Projects and University Chapters (2016–2019) and Entrepreneurial Development and Innovation (2019–2022).

## Action

- I designed a strategy for multilevel, mutually beneficial agreements among university presidents, students, and business leaders, featuring a replicable model and standardized operating guidelines.
- I defined an activation metric: a chapter was considered functional only when it engaged its student community through events, calls for participation, or joint programs.
- I managed outreach efforts on a campus-by-campus basis across two regions: 17 initiatives in La Laguna and 15 in Mérida and Yucatán (agreements, swearing-in ceremonies, academic departments, and special mentions).
- I expanded the system to include public impact: the anti-corruption campaign “Yo No Doy Mordida” (signing of the decree on Paseo Montejo, 2017) and the #ParticipoVotoExijo university tour promoting civic engagement.

## Results

- 32 formal partnerships with ~18 institutions nationwide (17 in La Laguna + 15 in Mérida and Yucatán), verified by name in the SSOT.
- Active chapters organizing their own events: ranging from the recruitment of 100 students (Gravity Rally Games, 2015) to forums such as #MXconFuturo (2018).
- A system sustained over 7 years and 3 vice-presidencies, with a smooth handover of operations between leadership transitions.

My outreach work in Mérida, including a networking event in collaboration with INCmty, put my name on the ecosystem’s radar and led to my next step: serving as national director of the HEINEKEN Green Challenge.

Proof that the model did not depend on a single person.

## Reflection

Being part of COPARMEX was truly important to me. I’ve always said it: one of my schools of life was the Tecnológico de Monterrey, and another was COPARMEX.

COPARMEX definitely taught me not only what it means to start a business, but also what it means to be a responsible entrepreneur: to understand that an entrepreneur’s commitment to their community never ceases to exist or to grow.

Having the opportunity to serve as vice president of COPARMEX Youth for more than five years was an honor and a pleasure. It truly left a lasting mark on the way I view business and recognize the importance of the country’s economic development without sacrificing social well-being.

It really was an incredible experience.
