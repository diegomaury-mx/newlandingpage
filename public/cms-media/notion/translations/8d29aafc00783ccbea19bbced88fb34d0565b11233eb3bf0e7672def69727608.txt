The HEINEKEN Green Challenge taught me something that I now use as a guiding principle: when an initiative doesn’t resonate, pushing harder rarely solves the problem. You have to redesign the system. For years, I thought success was measured by registrations, mentors, partners, states involved, and final results. Those numbers matter, and in this case, they were clear: more than 3,200 projects reached, more than 9,900 entrepreneurs registered, more than 100 mentors coordinated per edition, a team that grew from 6 to nearly 30 people, and a Southeast region that went from low participation to leading state-by-state registrations. But the real lesson lay beneath the surface: the talent was already there. What was missing was an entry point tailored to their reality. The Southeast didn’t need more noise; it needed relevance, trust, the right channels, regional autonomy, and an operation capable of sustaining growth without relying on a single person putting out fires. That’s when I realized that open innovation doesn’t live in the final pitch or the photo with the winners. It lives in the decisions that allow an entrepreneur to understand, trust, participate, and move forward. Scaling innovation isn’t about making the call for entries bigger. It’s about building the path so that potential can emerge.
